## 2. CUSTOMER SEGMENTS (PRIORITIZED)

### 2.1 Primary Target: Mid-Market Electrical Contractors

**Profile:**
- **Company Size:** 5-15 electricians (typically 8-12)
- **Annual Revenue:** $800K - $2.5M
- **Geographic:** Vancouver/Lower Mainland (60%), Victoria (15%), Interior BC (25%)
- **Work Mix:** 60% service/upgrade, 30% residential new construction, 10% light commercial
- **Tech Savvy:** Medium (use QuickBooks, smartphones, but no field management software)
- **Decision Maker:** Owner/operator (also works in field)

**Pain Points (Validated):**
1. **Documentation time waste:** 25-40 min/panel manually = $13K-20K/year lost productivity
2. **Quote preparation delays:** 2-3 days turnaround, lose 20-30% of bids to faster competitors
3. **Apprentice supervision overhead:** Journeymen spend 1.5-2 hr/day answering questions = $15K-20K/year
4. **Callback rework:** 4% callback rate costs $3,750-5,000/year
5. **Knowledge loss:** Senior electricians retiring take 30 years expertise with them
6. **ESA/TSBC inspection failures:** 15-20% failure rate = $5,400-8,100/year in re-inspection costs

**Buying Journey:**
- **Awareness:** Hear about it from apprentice, see demo at trade show, peer recommendation
- **Consideration:** Free trial (30 days), pilot with 2-3 electricians
- **Decision:** Owner + champion electrician review results after pilot
- **Purchase:** Owner approves <$500/month without external approval
- **Rollout:** 4-month gradual adoption (champion → pilot → training → mandatory)

**Annual Value:**
- **Subscription:** $39 × 10 users × 12 months = $4,680
- **Year 1 Total:** $4,680 + $2,000 training = $6,680
- **LTV (5 years @ 80% retention):** ~$18,720

**CAC Target:** <$2,000 (3.6 month payback)

#### Persona 2: **"Mike the Journeyman"** (Champion/Influencer)
- **Age:** 30-40
- **Role:** Lead journeyman electrician (FSR - Field Safety Representative)
- **Background:** 10-15 years experience, supervises apprentices, does complex jobs
- **Goals:** Do quality work efficiently, teach apprentices, avoid callbacks, pass inspections
- **Pains:** Constantly answering apprentice questions, re-documenting work for quotes/permits, inspection failures from apprentice mistakes
- **Tech Comfort:** High (uses smartphone for everything, watches YouTube tutorials)
- **Decision Influence:** High - if journeymen hate it, app fails regardless of owner's decision
- **Adoption Criteria:** Faster than current method, works offline, actually catches mistakes
- **Quote:** *"I spend 2 hours a day answering the same questions. If the app can answer them, I'm in."*

#### Persona 3: **"Alex the Apprentice"** (End User/Learner)
- **Age:** 20-28
- **Role:** Level 1-3 electrical apprentice
- **Background:** Learning trade, studying for Red Seal exam, works under journeyman supervision

- **Pains:** Code book is overwhelming, afraid to ask "dumb" questions, voltage drop calculations confusing, exam stress
- **Tech Comfort:** Very high (smartphone native, uses apps for everything)
- **Decision Influence:** Low on purchase, but high on daily adoption
- **Adoption Criteria:** Actually helps learn (not just gives answers), doesn't get them in trouble, respected by journeymen

#### Persona 4: **"Sarah the Inspector"** (Regulatory Influencer)

- **Pains:** Same violations repeatedly, contractors unprepared for inspection, outdated knowledge of 2021 CEC amendments
- **Tech Comfort:** Medium (uses tablet for inspection reports)
- **Decision Influence:** Indirect - contractors want to pass HER inspections
- **Value Perception:** If app improves first-time pass rates, she'll informally recommend it
- **Quote:** *"If contractors used a pre-inspection checklist, my job would be 50% easier and safer."*

#### Persona 5: **"Tom the Property Manager"** (End Customer Influencer)
- **Age:** 35-50
- **Role:** Manages 20-50 residential/commercial properties
- **Background:** Business/facilities background, hires electricians frequently
- **Goals:** Fast response, accurate quotes, documented work for insurance/warranty
- **Pains:** Contractors who take 3 days to send quotes, poor documentation for warranty claims, surprise costs
- **Tech Comfort:** High (uses property management software, expects digital reports)
- **Decision Influence:** Indirect - prefers contractors who provide professional documentation
- **Value Perception:** Will pay 5-10% premium for same-day professional reports
- **Quote:** *"I hire the contractor who responds fastest with a detailed quote. If they send a PDF in 2 hours, they get the job."*

### 3.4 Competitive Moats (Defensibility)

**Moat 1: Electrical Domain Expertise**
- **Barrier:** Takes years to understand electrical workflow, BC code, inspection requirements
- **ElectriScribe advantage:** Built by understanding apprentice→journeyman→inspector→contractor workflow
- **Competitor challenge:** ServiceTitan would need to rebuild for electrical; Fieldwire lacks domain depth

**Moat 2: AI Training Data**
- **Barrier:** Panel OCR requires thousands of labeled BC electrical panel images
- **ElectriScribe advantage:** First-mover data accumulation (every customer photo improves model)
- **Competitor challenge:** Can't build equivalent OCR without years of data collection

**Moat 3: Code Compliance Database**
- **Barrier:** CEC 2021 + BC amendments + local jurisdiction variations require electrical engineer expertise
- **ElectriScribe advantage:** Partnership with BC-licensed Professional Engineers for validation
- **Competitor challenge:** Liability risk of providing wrong code advice prevents entry

**Moat 4: Network Effects (Weak But Growing)**
- **Barrier:** Apprentices share "how to use ElectriScribe" tips in trade school WhatsApp groups
- **ElectriScribe advantage:** Viral adoption through apprentice cohorts
- **Competitor challenge:** Electricians are tribal - hard to break into established community

**Moat 5: Switching Costs (After 6 Months)**
- **Barrier:** Historical panel database, team knowledge base, documented procedures
- **ElectriScribe advantage:** More data captured = higher switching cost
- **Competitor challenge:** Must be 10× better to justify re-documenting 2 years of work

### 4.3 Pricing Psychology

**Why $39/user (not $40 or $35)?**
- **Charm pricing:** $39 feels significantly less than $40 psychologically
- **Competitive anchor:** Same base price as Fieldwire (but much more value)
- **Volume discount path:** $42 → $39 → $36 creates clear incentive to add users

**Why $49 for Solo (not $39)?**
- **Tiering separation:** Needs to feel distinctly different from Team tier
- **Lower LTV:** Solo customers churn faster, need higher monthly revenue
- **Upgrade incentive:** When solo electrician hires 2nd person, total cost drops from $49 → $84 (2 × $42), only $35 increase for 2× users

**Why $750 for Enterprise (not per-user)?**
- **Simplicity:** Large contractors hate per-seat licensing (complicated approvals)
- **Predictability:** Fixed cost for budgeting
- **Encourages adoption:** "Everyone can use it, we already paid for it"
- **Value alignment:** $750/month = $9,000/year for 20+ electricians = $450/user/year = reasonable for enterprise

## 5. GO-TO-MARKET STRATEGY (CONTRACTOR-FIRST APPROACH)

### 5.1 GTM Phases (Bottom-Up Adoption)

**Phase 1: Seed (Months 1-6) - Prove Product-Market Fit**

**Goal:** 5 paying customers, 50 active users, validated value delivery

**Tactics:**
1. **Direct Outreach to Warm Network**
   - Leveraging existing electrician relationships
   - IBEW Local 213 connections
   - Personal demos to 3-5 target contractors

2. **Free Pilot Program**
   - Offer 3-month free pilot to 5 contractors
   - Conditions: Weekly feedback calls, testimonial if successful
   - Success metric: 80% of pilots convert to paid

3. **Apprentice Viral Seeding**
   - Give free Solo accounts to 20 apprentices at BC trade schools
   - Encourage them to share with peers (referral bonus: Free 3 months for both)
   - Apprentices influence their employers to adopt Team tier

## 7. MARKET ENTRY RISKS & MITIGATIONS

### 7.1 Top 5 Risks

**Risk 1: Adoption Resistance (Senior Electricians Refuse to Use App)**
- **Probability:** HIGH (30-40% of journeymen resist new tech)
- **Impact:** HIGH (if journeymen don't adopt, app fails)
- **Mitigation:**
  - ✅ Gradual rollout (champion → pilot → full team over 4 months)
  - ✅ Grandfather clause (senior electricians can opt-out, but apprentices must use)
  - ✅ Compensation tie-in (submit in app = counts for commission/bonus)
  - ✅ Prove speed advantage (faster than paper by week 3)

**Risk 2: Offline Connectivity (App Fails in Basements/Rural Areas)**
- **Probability:** MEDIUM (20% of jobs have connectivity issues)
- **Impact:** CRITICAL (if app requires internet, unusable on job sites)
- **Mitigation:**
  - ✅ Offline-first architecture (WatermelonDB local storage)
  - ✅ Auto-sync when connectivity restored
  - ✅ Paper backup option (scan handwritten notes, app processes later)
  - ✅ Extensive testing in dead zones (basements, metal buildings, rural BC)

**Risk 3: Code Compliance Liability (App Gives Wrong Advice, Fire/Injury Results)**
- **Probability:** LOW (but catastrophic if occurs)
- **Impact:** CRITICAL (lawsuit, reputation destruction, business end)
- **Mitigation:**
  - ✅ Clear disclaimers ("For reference only, electrician must verify")
  - ✅ Professional Engineer validation (BC P.Eng stamps complex calculations)
  - ✅ Insurance: $5M E&O policy covering software advice
  - ✅ Conservative recommendations (when in doubt, suggest safer option)
  - ✅ Code version tracking ("Based on CEC 2021 + BC amendments 2024")
  - ✅ User agreement: "Electrician is responsible for code compliance"

**Risk 4: Competitor Response (ServiceTitan Adds Electrical Features)**
- **Probability:** MEDIUM (ServiceTitan may copy successful features)
- **Impact:** MEDIUM (but ElectriScribe has 2-3 year head start)
- **Mitigation:**
  - ✅ Speed to market (launch fast, iterate based on real feedback)
  - ✅ AI moat (panel OCR requires 1000s of BC-specific training images)
  - ✅ Community moat (apprentice viral adoption hard to replicate)
  - ✅ Focus on mid-market (ServiceTitan won't prioritize 5-15 employee contractors)
  - ✅ Partnership option (become electrical module FOR ServiceTitan)

**Risk 5: QuickBooks Integration Breaks (Intuit Changes API)**
- **Probability:** MEDIUM (APIs change, deprecations happen)
- **Impact:** HIGH (60%+ of customers rely on QB integration)
- **Mitigation:**
  - ✅ Official QuickBooks Partner (advance notice of API changes)
  - ✅ API version monitoring (alert when deprecation announced)
  - ✅ Fallback: CSV export (manual import to QB if API breaks)
  - ✅ Diversify integrations (add Xero, FreshBooks in Year 2)

### 7.2 Market Entry Lessons from Failed Competitors

**Example 1: TradesmenApp (2017-2019, Shut Down)**
- **Mistake:** Built for all trades (HVAC, plumbing, electrical, roofing)
- **Result:** Lacked depth in any one trade, couldn't compete with specialists
- **Lesson:** **Focus on electrical-only**, resist temptation to expand to other trades too soon

**Example 2: PanelDoc (2018-2020, Acquired for <$1M)**
- **Mistake:** Web-only (not mobile), required internet connection
- **Result:** Electricians couldn't use on job sites (no WiFi in basements)
- **Lesson:** **Mobile-first + offline-first is non-negotiable**

**Example 3: CodeChecker (2019-2021, Pivoted to B2C)**
- **Mistake:** Marketed to homeowners (DIY electrical advice)
- **Result:** Liability concerns, electricians hated it (gave homeowners false confidence)
- **Lesson:** **B2B only**, don't cannibalize professional electrician market
